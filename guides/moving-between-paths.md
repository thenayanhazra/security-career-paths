# Moving between paths

*Last reviewed: November 2026*

Few careers stay on one path. Most people change direction at least once, and the moves are easier than they look because so much carries over. This page lists the common moves and what each one needs.

## How to make the move

1. **Do a piece of the new work in your current job.** A SOC analyst who writes detection rules, or a developer who leads a threat-modelling session, has started the move already.
2. **Tell your manager.** Internal moves are the easiest. Many organisations would rather keep you than hire someone new.
3. **Close the biggest gap first.** Use the "what to add" column above and the target path's page.
4. **Show the work publicly.** A blog post or small tool in the new area gives external employers something to see.

## When to specialise and when to stay broad

Early in a career, breadth helps: you learn how the parts fit together. After a few years, depth gets you hired, because teams want someone who is the best at something. Later, breadth matters again for architecture, Staff+ engineering and leadership. The most effective senior people are "T-shaped": deep in one area, conversant in most.
