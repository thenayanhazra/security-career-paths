# Staying current

*Last reviewed: November 2026*

Security changes faster than most fields. New vulnerabilities appear daily, attacker techniques shift, and whole areas such as AI security did not exist as job titles a few years ago. Keeping up does not mean reading everything. It means a small, steady habit and a few sources you trust.

## A habit that works

- **Daily, ten minutes:** skim one news source and the [CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog). Ask of each item: would this affect anywhere I have worked?
- **Weekly, an hour:** read one long-form intrusion report or research write-up properly, from start to finish.
- **Monthly:** watch one conference talk in your path and one outside it.
- **Yearly:** reread your path's page here and check whether the skills it lists still match the jobs being advertised.

Reading is not the goal. After a good report, try to reproduce one technique in your lab or write one detection for it.

## Newsletters and news

| Source | Why it is worth your time |
|---|---|
| [tl;dr sec](https://tldrsec.com/) | A weekly digest of the best technical security writing, strong on AppSec, cloud and AI |
| [SANS NewsBites](https://www.sans.org/newsletters/newsbites/) | Twice-weekly news with short comments from experienced practitioners on what matters |
| [SANS Internet Storm Center](https://isc.sans.edu/) | Daily diaries from analysts on what they are seeing right now |
| [Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/) | Investigative reporting on cybercrime and the people behind it |

## Podcasts

| Podcast | Why it is worth your time |
|---|---|
| [Risky Business](https://risky.biz/) | A weekly news discussion that explains why each story matters, not just what happened |
| [Darknet Diaries](https://darknetdiaries.com/) | Long-form stories of real attacks and the people involved. A good way to build intuition about how incidents unfold |

## Reports worth reading in full

- [The DFIR Report](https://thedfirreport.com/): real intrusions reconstructed step by step
- [Google Project Zero blog](https://googleprojectzero.blogspot.com/): deep analysis of real vulnerabilities
- [MITRE ATLAS case studies](https://atlas.mitre.org/): documented attacks on AI systems

## Conferences

- [BSides](https://bsides.org/) events are local, cheap and welcoming. Go to one, and once you have something to say, submit a talk.
- [DEF CON](https://defcon.org/) and Black Hat ([talks on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@BlackHatOfficialYT)) publish many past talks free online. Watching talks from previous years is one of the best free training resources there is.
- Each path has its own specialist events, from ICS security summits to privacy and AI governance conferences. Ask people already in the path which ones they rate.

## Avoiding overload

- Pick two or three sources and drop the rest. Ten sources skimmed beat thirty unread.
- Favour primary sources such as vendor advisories, original research and incident reports over commentary about them.
- Be wary of anything that makes a threat sound new and terrifying without saying what to do about it.
