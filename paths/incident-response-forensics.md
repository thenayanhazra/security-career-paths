# Incident response and digital forensics

*Last reviewed: November 2026*

> Work out what happened, stop it spreading, recover, and make sure it does not happen the same way again.

When an incident is confirmed, responders take over. They scope the damage, contain the attacker, collect evidence, and guide the organisation back to normal. Forensic analysts reconstruct events from disks, memory, logs and network traffic, sometimes to a standard that holds up in court.

## What the work involves

- Leading or supporting the response to ransomware, account compromise, data theft and insider incidents
- Collecting and preserving evidence without destroying it
- Analysing disk images, memory captures and logs to build a timeline
- Reverse engineering malware, at the more specialised end
- Writing reports for technical teams, executives, lawyers and sometimes regulators
- Running exercises so the organisation is ready before the next incident

## Roles along the way

| Level | Typical titles | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Junior incident responder, DFIR analyst | Collect and analyse evidence under direction |
| Mid | Incident responder, forensic analyst, malware analyst | Run investigations; handle evidence; brief stakeholders |
| Senior | Incident response lead, DFIR manager, principal consultant | Lead major incidents; manage crises; set readiness programmes |

**Framework names:** NICE *Incident Response*, *Digital Forensics*, *Digital Evidence Analysis*; ECSF *Cyber Incident Responder*, *Digital Forensics Investigator*.

## Skills that matter

- Windows and Linux internals: where evidence lives and what normal looks like
- Forensic tools for disk, memory and timeline analysis
- Log analysis at scale
- Evidence handling and chain of custody
- Clear thinking and communication under pressure

## How people get in

Most responders spend a year or more in a SOC or in system administration first. Consultancies that offer incident response retainers hire analysts and train them across many organisations' incidents, which builds experience quickly. Law enforcement digital forensics units are another route, with their own entry requirements.

## Learn for free

- [13Cubed](https://www.youtube.com/@13cubed): clear, practical videos on Windows and memory forensics
- [The DFIR Report](https://thedfirreport.com/): real intrusions, step by step
- [Volatility](https://github.com/volatilityfoundation/volatility3): the standard open-source memory forensics framework
- [NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r3/final): incident response recommendations aligned to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

## Practise

- [CyberDefenders](https://cyberdefenders.org/) and [Blue Team Labs Online](https://blueteamlabs.online/): forensic challenges with real artefacts
- Capture memory and disk images from your own lab machines after running attacks against them, then investigate
