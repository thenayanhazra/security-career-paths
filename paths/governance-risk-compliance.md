# Governance, risk and compliance

*Last reviewed: November 2026*

> Turn standards, regulations and risk into decisions an organisation can make and evidence it can show.

GRC professionals decide what "secure enough" means for an organisation and check that it is achieved. They write policies, assess risk, manage audits and certifications, oversee suppliers and report to leadership. It is the largest non-engineering path in security and one of the best entry points for career changers.

## What the work involves

- Writing and maintaining security policies and standards
- Running risk assessments and keeping the risk register honest
- Preparing for and managing audits: ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2, PCI DSS, sector regulators
- Assessing third-party and supplier risk
- Testing that controls work, not just that they are documented
- Reporting security posture to executives and boards
- Keeping track of new regulation

## Roles along the way

| Level | Typical titles | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | GRC analyst, compliance analyst, IT auditor (junior) | Collect evidence; maintain policies; support audits |
| Mid | GRC manager, risk analyst, IT auditor, third-party risk lead | Own frameworks and audits; run risk assessments |
| Senior | Head of GRC, security risk director, audit director | Set risk appetite with leadership; report to the board |

**Framework names:** NICE *Cybersecurity Policy and Planning*, *Security Control Assessment*, *Technology Program Auditing*, *Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management*; ECSF *Cybersecurity Risk Manager*, *Cybersecurity Auditor*, *Cyber Legal, Policy and Compliance Officer*.

## Skills that matter

- Knowledge of the major frameworks and how they relate
- Clear, precise writing
- Enough technical understanding to question engineers and spot weak evidence
- Risk reasoning: likelihood, impact, and what the organisation will accept
- Stakeholder management and diplomacy

## How people get in

Many come from audit, accounting, legal, project management or IT. Graduates join audit and consulting firms' technology risk practices. Technical people who enjoy writing and influence also move across. The fastest-growing GRC tools and teams increasingly automate evidence collection, so scripting and data skills are becoming an advantage.

## Learn for free

- [NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework)
- [NIST SP 800-53](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final): the most detailed public security control catalogue
- [CIS Critical Security Controls](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls): a prioritised, practical control set
- [ISO/IEC 27001 overview](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001): the standard itself is paid, but its structure is well documented

## Practise

- Write a security policy set for a fictional company of 200 people, mapped to the CIS Controls
- Run a risk assessment on a real open-source project or a small organisation you know, with their permission
- Map one framework to another, for example CIS Controls to ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A

## Certifications

Starting: Security+ or CC. Building: ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Implementer or Lead Auditor, CGRC. Advanced: CISA, CRISC, CISM, CISSP. See [Certifications](../guides/certifications.md).
