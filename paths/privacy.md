# Privacy

*Last reviewed: November 2026*

> Make sure personal data is collected, used and shared lawfully, fairly and securely, and that people's rights over it are respected.

Privacy overlaps with security but is not the same thing: a system can be perfectly secure and still use data in ways people never agreed to. Privacy professionals interpret data protection law, assess new uses of data, handle individuals' requests and breaches, and increasingly work with engineers to build privacy into products.

## What the work involves

- Running data protection impact assessments
- Maintaining records of processing and data maps
- Handling individuals' requests to access, correct or delete their data
- Managing personal data breaches and notifications
- Advising product teams on privacy by design
- Reviewing contracts and international data transfers

## Roles along the way

| Level | Typical titles | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Privacy analyst, privacy operations specialist | Handle requests; maintain records; support assessments |
| Mid | Privacy manager, privacy engineer, privacy counsel | Run assessments; advise teams; manage breaches |
| Senior | Data protection officer, chief privacy officer | Own the privacy programme; engage regulators |

**Framework names:** NICE *Privacy Compliance*; ECSF *Cyber Legal, Policy and Compliance Officer*.

## Skills that matter

- Data protection law in the jurisdictions you work in, such as the GDPR, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act or US state laws
- Assessment and judgment: balancing purpose, necessity and risk
- Clear advice in plain language
- For privacy engineers: data flows, anonymisation and pseudonymisation, and access controls

## How people get in

Lawyers, compliance professionals and GRC analysts move into privacy most often. Privacy engineering is a growing technical branch that draws software and data engineers.

## Practise

- Write a data protection impact assessment for a real or fictional app
- Build a data map for a small system and identify where personal data could be minimised
