# AI governance and assurance

*Last reviewed: November 2026*

> Make an organisation's use of AI accountable, compliant and auditable, without stopping it from getting the benefits.

As AI moves into decisions about credit, hiring, healthcare and security itself, organisations need people who can classify AI systems by risk, set policy, meet new regulation, assess suppliers and give boards an honest picture. AI governance sits where GRC, privacy, ethics and technology meet.

## What the work involves

- Building and maintaining an inventory of AI systems and how risky each one is
- Writing AI policy and acceptable-use standards
- Running AI impact and risk assessments
- Mapping obligations under regulation such as the EU AI Act
- Implementing management systems against ISO/IEC 42001
- Auditing AI systems and the controls around them
- Reporting AI risk to executives and boards

## Roles along the way

| Level | Typical titles | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | AI governance analyst, responsible AI analyst | Maintain inventories; support assessments; track regulation |
| Mid | AI governance manager, AI risk manager, AI auditor | Run assessments and audits; own policy; advise product teams |
| Senior | Head of AI governance, chief AI risk officer, responsible AI lead | Set AI risk appetite with leadership; report to the board |

**Framework names:** no AI-specific role yet. Closest: NICE *Cybersecurity Policy and Planning*, *Privacy Compliance* and *Technology Program Auditing*; ECSF *Cybersecurity Risk Manager*, *Cybersecurity Auditor* and *Cyber Legal, Policy and Compliance Officer*.

## Skills that matter

- The main frameworks and laws: NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act and local rules where you work
- Risk assessment and control design, from GRC
- Enough technical understanding of how models and agents work to ask engineers the right questions
- Clear writing for non-specialists
- Keeping up: this area changed substantially during 2025 and 2026

## How people get in

From GRC, privacy, audit, legal and risk management, usually by taking on AI within an existing role. Technical people who prefer policy and influence also move across.

## Trade-offs

- **Shifting rules and guidance:** regulatory enforcement timelines, national standards and best practices are still settling, so policies need regular revision.
- **Shadow AI everywhere:** business teams adopt AI tools far faster than formal review processes move, making practical guardrails harder than writing rules.
- **Bridging two worlds:** lawyers expect precision while ML engineers want speed; without enough technical grounding, governance teams get bypassed.

## Learn for free

- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) and the [AI RMF Playbook](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/playbook/)
- [EU AI Act Explorer](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/ai-act-explorer/): the regulation, searchable article by article
- [OWASP AI Exchange](https://owaspai.org/): open-source AI security and governance guidance feeding into international standards
- [CSA AI Controls Matrix](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/artifacts/ai-controls-matrix-v1-1): controls mapped across AI security and governance frameworks
- [OECD AI Policy Observatory](https://oecd.ai/): policies and incidents from around the world
- [AI Security Governance](https://github.com/thenayanhazra/ai-security-governance): a risk framework, 34 controls mapped to NIST, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act, OWASP and MITRE ATLAS, and a board reporting template

## Practise

- Build an AI inventory and risk classification for a fictional organisation using five or six realistic AI use cases
- Classify each use case under the EU AI Act and justify your reasoning
- Review a third-party AI vendor's public security and privacy documentation and draft a supplier assurance checklist covering data retention, training-data use, model updates and incident notification
- Write a one-page AI risk briefing for a board, with indicators and a decision to take

## Ready to apply

You are ready to interview for an AI governance and assurance role when you can:

- Classify an AI use case by risk tier under the EU AI Act and map practical controls from NIST AI RMF or ISO/IEC 42001
- Ask sharp technical questions about training data, RAG data sources, agent permissions, evaluation results and fallback behaviour
- Design a lightweight intake and vendor-review workflow that enables safe adoption instead of blocking every request
- Brief senior stakeholders on AI risks, regulatory obligations and key risk indicators in one page of plain language

## Certifications

Building: IAPP AIGP. Advanced: ISACA AAISM (for security managers holding CISM or CISSP), AAIA (for auditors) and AAIR (for risk professionals). ISC2 announced an AI security certification in July 2026, expected to pilot in late 2026. See [Certifications](../guides/certifications.md).
